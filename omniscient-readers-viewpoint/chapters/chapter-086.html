The Square Circle

Han Sooyoung and Yoo Sangah barely spoke throughout the drive along the dimensional road.

That gave me time to look out the window and sort through my thoughts. Things I had to do, and things I wanted to do.

Beside me, Uriel—who had been muttering something with a grave expression the whole time—was now snoring in her sleep. Secretive Plotter was still unconscious, half leaning against her.

It felt strange to see the two oldest presences in my channel sleeping so defenselessly.

Watching me in the rearview mirror, Han Sooyoung said,

“What are you grinning at? You’d better be ready to explain yourself when we get back.”

Explain. It was obvious what I would have to explain.

“You’ll only get one chance.”

Yoo Sangah’s gentle smile was terrifying.

“We’re here.”

Before long, the Ferrarghini stopped.

We were in Seoul.

●

A little later, I was sitting in front of my companions.

Faces I had missed. The faces I had longed to see were there.

<Kim Dokja’s Company>, including Lee Gilyoung, Shin Yoosung, Jung Heewon and Lee Jihye, who had gone through the scenarios with me. Lee Seolhwa and Gong Pildu, who had protected Seoul while I was away. Farther off, at the end of the reception room, I could also see my mother and the Wanderers.

I looked over their faces, then began by bowing at a ninety-degree angle.

“I’m sorry.”

“For what?”

“For everything I’ve done. I’m sorry for all of it.”

“Hmm… Well, all right.”

…What? Weren’t they angry with me?

I didn’t understand, but I decided this was a good thing. There was so much I had to explain.

“There are a few things I’d like to explain—”

“First, tell us whose kid that is.”

Gong Pildu snapped at me. Following his gaze, I saw Secretive Plotter floating beside me, enclosed in a transparent sphere.

[The target’s Stories are currently unstable.]

Since he still hadn’t regained consciousness, his excessive use of probability seemed to have caused a problem. In other words, he couldn’t explain himself. When I didn’t answer, Gong Pildu’s eyes filled with rage.

“I protected Seoul because you said you were going out to clear scenarios, and you have the nerve to come back after having a kid?”

His voice carried all the resentment of a father fated to live apart from his family.

“Look, I can see what you’ve misunderstood, but—”

“Which one?”

Gong Pildu cast a frightened glance at Yoo Sangah.

“…Don’t tell me?”

He met her smiling eyes, then shook his head.

“…Right. Of course not. So it’s her, then?”

“Do you want to die?”

Han Sooyoung snarled, making Gong Pildu flinch. I seized the opening.

“Isn’t it a bit of a stretch to assume someone gave birth to him in the first place? Does he look like a baby to you?”

“The kid Han Myungoh gave birth to grew up in no time, too.”

Han Myungoh went pale at that.

“I’d rather you didn’t bring that up.”

“What bothers me more is that the brat has the exact same face as that insufferable bastard.”

Gong Pildu glanced toward the edge of the reception room.

Yoo Joonghyuk sat there with his whole body swathed in bandages, his legs crossed and his eyes wide. He fixed me with his usual terrifying stare.

—Kim Dokja. What on earth are you thinking?

I heaved a sigh.

“Of course he looks exactly like that insufferable bastard. He is that insufferable bastard.”

Silence fell over the room. Gong Pildu stared at me as if asking what kind of nonsense I was spouting.

It looked like this was going to take a while.

“There’s more than one Yoo Joonghyuk in this world. …I suppose I should start there.”

●

I began explaining things step by step, starting with what had happened at the end of the Great War of Saints and Demons.

How I had summoned the Nameless Mist, the Outer God that had destroyed the Demon Realm, to stop the Apocalypse Dragon. (Gong Pildu: “Are you insane?”)

How I had encountered Secretive Plotter in the process and been kidnapped by him. (Lee Seolhwa: “Oh my.”)

How that Secretive Plotter had turned out to be Yoo Joonghyuk, who had lived through 1,863 rounds. (Jang Hayoung: “…What does that even mean?”)

How I had made a contract with him concerning the Outer Gods. (Shin Yoosung: “…I thought you might, ajusshi.”)

How I had secretly joined the Giant Story 「Journey to the West」 without telling my companions. (Lee Jihye: “You’re really not cut out for acting, ajusshi.”)

How the Yoo Joonghyuk of the 1863rd round had fought the Yoo Joonghyuk of the 999th round. (Jung Heewon: “Just how many Yoo Joonghyuk-ssis are there?”)

How this worldline’s Yoo Joonghyuk, whom we had thought was on his third life, had actually been on his 1,864th. (Han Myungoh: “Are you perhaps bluffing your way through this because you can’t explain it yourself?”)

How I had barely cleared 「Journey to the West」 with my companions’ help, only to be kidnapped by Secretive Plotter again. (Yoo Sangah: a wordless sigh.)

And how, there, I had met the Uriel of the 999th round, who had become an Outer God…

Having explained that much, I wondered what on earth I was even saying.

When I looked up, my companions were all standing there wearing much the same expression.

Han Myungoh was the first to respond.

“Ahem. So that’s what happened. I understand everything.”

…There was no way he did, was there?

Everyone looked at Han Myungoh. He added,

“You like dying and getting kidnapped.”

“…Um, I’m not the only one who doesn’t get it, right? What is any of this supposed to mean? There are three Masters, and what’s this about the 1864th round…?”

Of course they didn’t understand.

The very fact that the 1863rd round had split in two was complicated enough.

The Yoo Joonghyuk who had lived through the original 1863rd round had become Secretive Plotter.

The Yoo Joonghyuk who had lived through the altered 1863rd round had regressed and become the Yoo Joonghyuk we knew.

I could understand because I had read all of Ways of Survival, but naturally, it was incomprehensible to my companions. Clutching her head, Jung Heewon asked,

“What does any of that mean? Is Joonghyuk-ssi on his third round or his 1,864th?”

Perched on the wheeled bed, Yoo Joonghyuk answered curtly.

“I don’t know either.”

“What?”

“Because I don’t remember.”

I activated [Character List] and checked Yoo Joonghyuk’s information.

●

<Character Summary>

Character: Yoo Joonghyuk

Exclusive Attributes: Regressor <3rd Round> (Myth)…

●

To my astonishment, Yoo Joonghyuk’s attribute once again displayed ‘3rd Round.’

“The memories only return when I briefly borrow Kim Dokja’s Story. It’s like looking at someone else’s history.”

…I hadn’t imagined that was how it worked.

Yoo Joonghyuk continued.

“This is what I think. This worldline was originally the third round, and I am the Yoo Joonghyuk of the third round. But at some point in this worldline, my memories from living through the 1864th round were superimposed.”

Lee Seolhwa, who had been listening quietly, spoke up.

“…Does that make any sense? You’re saying the third round affected the 1863rd round, while the 1863rd round simultaneously affected the third… That’s logically impossible.”

“Logically, sure.”

Han Sooyoung finally stepped in.

“This only works in the form of sentences. It’s possible because this world was originally a ‘novel.’”

Han Sooyoung looked at Secretive Plotter inside his transparent sphere.

“It’s just like a ‘square circle’ or a ‘triangle whose interior angles add up to 720 degrees.’”

Lee Seolhwa tilted her head.

“But those things can’t exist, can they?”

“It’s more accurate to say you can’t imagine them. They can exist as sentences. The same goes for what’s happening now. It’s a time paradox to us, but it’s possible in the sentences of a novel. You just have to accept that it is. It’s a matter of acceptance, not understanding. Think of it this way: we’re inside a lousy novel. The original was a mess, so this is what we ended up with.”

Even now, I wanted to argue. But it was true, so I had nothing to say.

“If I’d been the author, I would’ve stopped at one or two twists in the worldlines. Readers don’t like stories this complicated. I bet even the constellations don’t know what’s going on.”

[The constellation ‘Abyssal Black Flame Dragon’ says his incarnation is clever.]

“A world with broken probability destroys itself. I know plenty of stories that ended like that. Worlds even their authors gave up on.”

It was something Han Sooyoung could say because she was an author. Perhaps she, too, had once given up on a world that way. Perhaps she regretted it even now.

The thought left me with a strange feeling.

The world created by tls123, the author of Ways of Survival, had become reality.

―Then what kind of ending would you like to see, dear reader? What kind of ending would be a happy ending for the protagonist?

Could the author really have entrusted us with a story they hadn’t managed to finish?

Scratching her cheek, Jung Heewon asked,

“So what’s your conclusion, Han Sooyoung?”

“We fight the bastards making us struggle through a world like this. The author, the Outer Gods, the dokkaebis—whoever they are.”

“So, the same as usual.”

“The original can go to hell. We have to reach an ending of our own. We can’t stay in this goddamn cesspit of scenarios forever.”

Yes. She was right about all of it.

Whether our enemy was the king of the Outer Gods or the <Bureau> didn’t matter.

[All the constellations of the nebula <Kim Dokja’s Company> shine.]

Whoever the enemy was, we had only one choice left.

Fight, win, and reach our answer.

“Dokja-ssi?”

At some point, everyone had turned to look at me.

Their eyes asked me to say something.

They were waiting to hear what we would do next, and how, and what we had to prepare. Of course, I had given it some thought.

But the first words wouldn’t come easily.

Perhaps it was the tension of knowing the end was near.

The fear that, after struggling to come this far, one wrong decision on my part might ruin everything. The pressure of having to walk a path that wasn’t in the original.

I opened and closed my mouth several times before finally speaking.

“Well…”

“That’s enough.”

It was Yoo Sangah who stopped me.

“Let’s get some rest today and talk again tomorrow. Everyone’s only just come back from a scenario.”

●

I spent that whole night making plans.

Several times, I was tempted to read the final version of Ways of Survival, but in the end, I didn’t. I wasn’t sure why, but I had a feeling.

The feeling that, the moment I read it, I would never escape that story’s shackles.

…

I fell into a deep sleep without even knowing when. My last memories were hazy. I seemed to have fallen asleep reading a book, and I seemed to remember drinking the warm tea Lee Gilyoung had brought me at some point. In any case, it was the first sweet sleep I’d had in a long time.

I thought I had a brief, happy dream.

A dream like something I had once discussed with Yoo Sangah. A world where all the scenarios had ended. My companions were chatting about everyday things. It was peaceful. So peaceful it didn’t feel like peace. The moment I saw Shin Yoosung and Lee Gilyoung smiling so brightly, I somehow realized.

「This is a dream.」

I bit down on my lip, and the dreamscape shook as though struck by an earthquake. Through my hazy consciousness, I slowly opened my eyes and sat up.

…What?

I couldn’t move.

And the faint earthquake I had felt in the dream was still going on.

I barely managed to open my eyes. My surroundings emerged from the murky darkness. Soft leather cradled my back and the back of my head.

“Hey, Kim Dokja’s waking up.”

“Put him back to sleep.”

I felt someone whack me on the head, and my consciousness faded again.

Through the haze, I heard a mischievous voice.

“It’s a workers’ revolution, you bastard.”

And when I opened my eyes again.

I was somewhere remote in the mountains.

I wondered how I had ended up here.

One: I was tied up tight.

Two: I had been dumped on a mountain I didn’t recognize.

No matter how I looked at it, I had been kidnapped. But who would dare infiltrate <Kim Dokja’s Company> to kidnap me? Which meant…

“…is…”

“…Dokja… untied…?”

“Oh? …”

Faint voices reached my ears.

Grunting, I worked my way out of the ropes and staggered toward the voices. After pushing through the dense mountain growth for about thirty seconds, I spotted a spacious campsite and my companions.

“Oh, here he comes on his own.”

Han Sooyoung waved at me with a grin.

“What are you staring at? Never seen a workers’ strike before?”

“No, what are you—”

“The air’s so niiiice. Lie down here too, Dokja-ssi.”

Jung Heewon lay sprawled beside Han Sooyoung, facing the sky. She moved her arms like flapping wings, making the fresh grass lie down and rise again, over and over. Han Sooyoung murmured solemnly,

“The grass lies down. It lies down faster than the wind.”

“Oh.”

“Jung Heewon lies down, too. She lies down faster than the wind, and rises before the wind.”

“You’re good at this.”

Watching this impromptu poetry recitation contest, with Jung Heewon enthusiastically chiming in, I asked in bewilderment,

“What on earth are you doing…?”

“It’s a poem of the workers’ revolution, you bastard.”

“You keep going on about this workers’ revolution—”

“Oh, let us rest. Do we have to spell everything out for you?”

I frowned at Han Sooyoung’s scolding.

We were here to rest?

“What are you talking about? Do you know what kind of situation we’re in?”

“What kind?”

Put that way, I had nothing to say. What kind of situation were we in?

[The nebula <Kim Dokja’s Company> has obtained the qualifications for the final scenario.]

[Time remaining until scenario entry: 28 days, 12 hours, 15 minutes and 7 seconds.]

I answered calmly, determined not to let her set the pace.

“We don’t have time for this. The final scenario is right around the corner.”

“That’s why we have to rest now. When else will we get a break like this?”

Han Sooyoung let out a series of sighs.

“Look around. Stop living with your nose buried in your smartphone. Do you really want to work even in a place like this?”

Only then did I take in the mountains around us.

A mountain lush with green. I didn’t know which one. Jirisan, Seoraksan, Hallasan… Whatever it was, it was beautiful. The sunlight was mild and the breeze cool. In short, perfect camping weather.

I hesitated.

“Of course I’m not saying we shouldn’t rest… Rest is good. But we should finish what needs doing first. Right now, we—”

“Wow, Dokja-ssi. You really think like an old fogey. Are all CEOs like this?”

Jung Heewon tapped my shin.

“When we rest, we just rest, boss-nim.”

My thoughts were tangled.

When I still didn’t answer, Han Sooyoung glared at me, then spoke flatly.

“Fine. You’re right. We can’t all rest, so someone has to do a scenario. You work, then.”

“What?”

“Go do one of those scenarios you love.”

At her words, I happened to glance up—and there really was a scenario window floating in the air.

[Sub-scenario—‘Worker’s Day Off’ has occurred!]

It was a scenario I had never heard of.

I opened the scenario window.

+

<Sub-scenario—Worker’s Day Off>

Classification: Sub

Difficulty: ???

Clear conditions: You are the representative of the nebula <Kim Dokja’s Company>. Your prolonged exploitation and harsh treatment have left the employees of <Kim Dokja’s Company> utterly exhausted. Deeply dissatisfied with you, their nebula’s representative, they are engaged in a labor dispute. As their representative, listen to their grievances and appease them. In consideration of your poor communication skills, your grievance-resolution target is five people.

Time limit: 12 hours

Reward: The trust of <Kim Dokja’s Company>’s employees.

Failure: Death(?)

+

Death? What kind of ridiculous scenario was this…?

I looked up. Biyoo cried, “Baat.”

Han Sooyoung grumbled at me.

“Honestly, that bastard only understands when you explain it as a scenario.”

*

Anxiously, I looked around.

My companions seemed happy. Han Sooyoung and Jung Heewon, who had been reciting one parody poem after another, were asleep on the hillside. Lee Gilyoung and Shin Yoosung stood nose to nose, growling at each other.

“Hey, Shin Yoosung. Let’s bet on who can catch the bigger dinner.”

“What do I get if I win?”

“How about the loser grants the winner a wish?”

“You’re on.”

“Be careful,” Yoo Sangah called after the children as they dashed into the forest.

There was a small stream on one side of the campsite. Gong Pildu had brought a fishing chair and was yawning as he cast his line. Sitting beside him, Han Myungoh was muttering something.

“Whenever I went out to sea, I’d catch black sea bream this big…”

I heard the cool rush of the stream and the chirping of mountain birds. The mountain’s luxuriant greenery seemed to blur together.

It still felt like a dream.

That tenderness made me uncomfortable, as though I were wearing clothes that didn’t fit. Was this all right? Could I really experience a moment like this already?

I looked for Yoo Joonghyuk.

This wasn’t right. He would agree with me. No doubt he was somewhere glaring at everyone. With that terrifying stare, he would be delivering a fussy lecture beginning, “At a time like this, you fools…!”

I found him quickly.

I was about to raise my hand and call him when I noticed something strange.

Ssssss.

Yoo Joonghyuk was cooking. He stood before a large griddle, his hands moving with dazzling skill as he grilled meat. Vegetables danced above a frying pan. [Breaking the Sky Swordsmanship], which could shatter the heavens, was chopping up vegetables and meat.

Forgetting to call him, I watched as if spellbound.

…What on earth was happening?

The next moment, Yoo Joonghyuk’s eyes turned toward me. His usual terrifying stare spoke to me. Even without [Omniscient Reader’s Viewpoint], I could tell. Those eyes clearly meant:

「You can stare all you want. You’re not getting any.」

That was what he was saying.

Beside him stood Yoo Mia and Lee Jihye, their eyes shining with curiosity.

“Now.”

Yoo Mia opened her mouth, and Yoo Joonghyuk moved his chopsticks with an impassive face. Like a mother bird, he placed pieces of meat in her mouth one by one. Yoo Mia beamed.

“It’s delicious, as always.”

Lee Jihye, who had been standing blankly beside them, opened her mouth too. Yoo Joonghyuk glanced at her, then put another piece of meat in Yoo Mia’s mouth. This happened four or five times. Lee Jihye clamped her mouth shut.

“Master, you’re really too much.”

Unable to stand it, Lee Jihye reached out with her own chopsticks. Yoo Joonghyuk’s frying pan followed the wondrous trajectory of [Red Phoenix Shunpo], avoiding her hand. Her face crumpled, and she finally lost her temper.

“So this is how you want to play?”

I couldn’t tell whether I had entered the world of Ways of Survival or 『There’s No Way the Cold, Handsome Regressor Yoo Joonghyuk Could Be Like This』.

Yoo Joonghyuk kept feeding Yoo Mia while evading Lee Jihye’s chopsticks, his expression as blank as ever, without so much as a twitch of an eyebrow. But there were things even that blank face revealed.

Only then did I realize. Yoo Joonghyuk was taking this gathering seriously.

「Why had Yoo Joonghyuk allowed this at a time like this?」

I wasn’t the only CEO of <Kim Dokja’s Company>. In many ways, Yoo Joonghyuk was far more stubborn than I was. He also had plenty of experience running groups like this.

And yet, he had readily joined this camping trip.

「Do you re al ly not know, Kim Dok ja?」

As the [Fourth Wall] spoke, sentences unfolded before my eyes.

「Author-nim, how about visiting the sea this round?」

It was a comment I had once posted.

I remembered Ways of Survival so well, yet had completely forgotten the comments I had left. Thinking back, there was one event Yoo Joonghyuk had rarely skipped despite repeating so many regressions.

「“Today, we rest.”」

Rest.

Something that had existed in the original Ways of Survival, but not in this world.

Whenever he passed a major hurdle, he would take his companions to a tourist destination on another planet. He claimed, of course, that he was going to obtain materials needed for the scenarios, but he didn’t force the others to do the same.

「“Master, come play with us!”」

「“Hey, Lee Jihye. Heh heh. Take a look at these abs. Even my Black Flame Dragon praised…”」

「“Are we eating Joonghyuk-ssi’s cooking today?”」

Even Yoo Joonghyuk had done that. What about me?

「…We need to prepare for the next scenario.」

I had always hurried, always been pressed for time. The farther into the scenarios we went, the worse it became. There was always another goal right in front of me. I acted as if the entire scenario would fall apart unless I took care of it. But thinking it over, the scenarios probably wouldn’t have been ruined even if I hadn’t done those things immediately.

“Hey, Lee Gilyoung! We agreed not to use skills here!”

“When did I agree to that? You should always do your best!”

The children’s voices came from afar as they returned with their catches.

Then Han Sooyoung’s voice rang through the valley.

“All right, the treasure hunt starts now! The prize is a Star Relic from the Black Flame Dragon!”

“What? Really? That Black Flame Dragon?”

“I hid it in the stream, so whoever finds it first gets it. Oh, you know skills are banned, right? There are some smaller prizes too—”

“The Star Relic’s mine!”

Department Manager Han Myungoh threw aside his fishing rod and jumped into the stream. A moment later, Jung Heewon grabbed him by the hair and sent him flying. Before I knew it, Lee Jihye and the children, back from their hunt, had jumped in too.

“Wait, Jihye-noona! That’s [Ghost Fleet]! You think we won’t notice if you summon it small?”

“Lee Gilyoung! Taming a diving beetle? That’s cheating!”

“You’re both disqualified!”

I couldn’t remember the last time I had seen everyone laughing together. Perhaps this was the first time. They could laugh even before the scenarios ended. They could chatter so happily, and talk to each other.

「And watching that scene, Kim Dokja felt strangely lonely.」

Could it be that all this time, I hadn’t understood anything about Ways of Survival—no, about my companions? Had I been so intoxicated by the dream of seeing the ending that I had missed all the countless sentences I needed to read to reach it?

[You have currently resolved 0 grievances.]

The scenario I had thought so trivial suddenly felt as difficult as a Giant Story.

I had perched on top of a parasol and was standing awkwardly when someone tapped my shoulder.

“How’s the scenario coming along?”

Yoo Sangah stood there, smiling. When I gave her a feeble smile, she continued.

“You’ve never been particularly good at communicating, Dokja-ssi. You were like that back at the company too.”

“…Was I?”

“You barely spoke to anyone at all.”

I had no answer to that merciless bombardment of facts. Thinking about it, it was only natural. I had lived without a single friend since childhood. I didn’t really know how to get along with people, and at company dinners, I only thought about escaping. I thought rereading Ways of Survival would be a better use of that time.

「Kim Dok ja has fri ends now.」

And one irritating inanimate object that insisted it was my friend.

Perched on the parasol, Yoo Sangah watched our companions at ease. Perhaps to suit the camping mood, she wore a casual dress and a broad-brimmed straw hat instead of her Buddhist robes today. Yoo Sangah fit in anywhere. She was wasted on <Kim Dokja’s Company> and its pathetic leader.

“Sangah-ssi, do you ever regret getting on the subway that day?”

I didn’t know why I had asked.

The first scenario was still vivid in my mind.

「If Yoo Sangah’s bicycle hadn’t been stolen that day.」

If she had started somewhere else, she wouldn’t have had to become an incarnation of <Olympus>. She wouldn’t have had to die. She wouldn’t have had to suffer the pain of reincarnation…

“No.”

I had never seen Yoo Sangah look so resolute.

“So don’t have regrets either, Dokja-ssi.”

“What? Regret what…?”

“Just. All of it.”

I didn’t know what to say. Even saying thank you, saying how grateful I was, felt as though it would only burden her. Ways of Survival hadn’t taught me what to say in a situation like this.

As if she understood, Yoo Sangah smiled and pointed at someone.

“Why don’t you try talking to that person first?”

“My concerns?”

“Yes, well… Do you have any complaints about working at the company…?”

The first person I approached was Lee Seolhwa. In field research clothes and wearing a small magnifier, she examined my face from every angle, as if she had found some unknown medicinal herb, then spoke.

“Hmm. Not really.”

She might say that, but there was no way she had no complaints.

“As the company’s representative, I always feel I owe you an apology. I know how well you looked after Seoul while I was gone.”

“Hmm.”

“And how hard it must have been…”

“Do you really think that? Don’t you actually think I had it easier in Seoul?”

Her sharp tone left me speechless.

“So you did think that. Was that a subtle dig at me?”

“No. Absolutely not.”

“I know the others were throwing themselves into dangerous scenarios. But that doesn’t mean staying in Seoul was easy.”

Lee Seolhwa lowered her head and resumed searching the undergrowth.

“It should be somewhere around here…”

「Lee Seolhwa had not had a single day of proper rest.」

Her Story was speaking for her.

「At the [Industrial Complex] her companions had left behind, Lee Seolhwa ran the wards and received patients alone. Injured incarnations flooded in every day. She watched them die, and as she watched them die, she thought of her companions.」

“I won’t be much help in the later scenarios. I know my own potential, and my constellation is only Historical-grade. But I did my best.”

The intensity of the Status surrounding Lee Seolhwa had certainly changed. Her combat power hadn’t grown like the others’, but how could I put it? Her mastery of her skills felt far deeper.

“As long as anyone in <Kim Dokja’s Company> is still breathing, I will save them. I can keep any of you from dying.”

In fact, Lee Seolhwa was growing as quickly as she had in any round of the original. If my guess was right, she would soon reach the realm of the Divine Doctor of Life and Death (生死神醫). She was indispensable to the ending I dreamed of.

“What sort of person was I in the book you read, Dokja-ssi?”

The sudden question caught me off guard.

“An important person.”

“How important, exactly?”

Lee Seolhwa had been a heroine in Ways of Survival. But I couldn’t very well confess that she had once been Yoo Joonghyuk’s lover. Yoo Joonghyuk didn’t seem to want that either… More than anything, I wasn’t sure whether it was a fair description of Lee Seolhwa at all. What sort of person had she really been?

“Well…”

Just as I was about to continue, Lee Seolhwa cried out in delight.

“Oh, found it!”

She held a small flower. Apparently, it was the herb she had been looking for. I recognized it immediately.

「White Ghostly Flame Flower (白燐華). The final ingredient of the Life and Death Pill (生死丸).」

It looked like an insignificant weed, and eating it on its own had no effect. Yet without that herb, the miraculous Life and Death Pill could never be made.

Lee Seolhwa beamed like a child. The scene before me had a vitality no sentence in Ways of Survival had ever possessed.

「This is Lee Seolhwa.」

So I stopped trying to recall the sentences of Ways of Survival. Instead, I spoke in my own clumsy words.

“You’re the best doctor I know.”

Even a child could have come up with a better compliment. Despite that, Lee Seolhwa smiled.

“Thank you, even if they’re empty words.”

“They’re not empty words—”

“Just wait. I’ll make those empty words true.”

As Lee Seolhwa left to look for another herb, I realized something. It hadn’t really been Ways of Survival she was curious about. Unlike me, she didn’t need a novel like that.

[You have currently resolved 0 grievances.]

I had made no progress in the scenario, but I didn’t feel bad.

“It’s not easy, is it?”

I turned. Yoo Sangah was there again.

“…No. It isn’t.”

“Of course not. If you could get through all the conversations you’ve put off as if you were clearing an event, this would be a novel, not reality.”

“You’re right.”

“But you still have to keep trying.”

I nodded.

“Who should I talk to next?”

“I’d like you to find them yourself this time, but I’ll help just once more.”

Making a show of shading her eyes with a hand, Yoo Sangah scanned our companions.

At that moment, I heard a message.

[The contract employees of <Kim Dokja’s Company> are currently harboring grievances.]

…Contract employees? Our nebula had those?

Then Yoo Sangah pointed.

“Try over there this time.”

The moment I looked, I realized who the ‘contract employees’ were.

A little later, I addressed the three people lined up before me.

“There’s something I’d like to tell you.”

If my total mental strength plummeted on this trip, it would be entirely Han Sooyoung’s fault.

Several particularly proactive members of <Kim Dokja’s Company> had already bought large sheets of paper and pens through the [Dokkaebi Bag]. Those dokkaebi bastards even charged coins for things like this…

Across the fire, Yoo Joonghyuk also seemed angry.

“I’m not doing this nonsense.”

…Though his reason for being angry seemed different from mine.

In any case, watching my companions huddle together on the ground, each writing away, stirred fresh feelings in me. It looked like a writing club for poor, friendless Kim Dokja.

As we each wrote our names on a sheet and passed it around, Lee Gilyoung raised his hand.

“Hyung, I bought this from the [Dokkaebi Bag]. Can I launch it?”

Shin Yoosung brightened at the toy in Lee Gilyoung’s hand.

“Oh! Isn’t that what people used to fly at the Han River?”

“That’s exactly what I was thinking of when I bought it.”

“Let me launch one too!”

“No. Buy your own. It cost 2,000 coins.”

Lee Gilyoung held a parachute helicopter. I had seen them a few times too. A toy that shot into the sky and lit up when you pulled its cord hard and let go.

What was unusual about Lee Gilyoung’s toy was that its four blades formed a large square.

…Still, 2,000 coins for something like that.

“All right, here goes!”

Lee Gilyoung launched the parachute helicopter into the air. It shot upward in an instant, spinning furiously and bathing the surroundings in light. Light scattered across the night sky like fireworks. Though my companions were used to sights far more spectacular, they looked impressed.

The helicopter’s blades spun rapidly, turning their square outline into a circle.

It looked just like a portal. A portal to the world we had lived in. Nostalgia for a world we could never return to, but which had unquestionably been there.

That was when a system message rang out in the air.

[The incarnation ‘Lee Gilyoung’ has used the item ‘Parachute Helicopter (Supersized Optical Screen)’!]

The spinning helicopter blades grew larger and larger, then began to take the form of a screen. Lee Jihye frowned.

“What? That was a hologram panel? We have to watch scenarios even here?”

“Lee Gilyoung, did you read the instructions properly…”

“No, it just said it was a helicopter, so…”

As Lee Gilyoung spoke, a slight tremor shook the area.

My companions’ expressions stiffened.

“What kind of bullshit is this now…?”

At Han Sooyoung’s words, everyone stared at the screen in the air.

The moment we saw the screen, we all understood the earthquake’s origin. It wasn’t an earthquake on the Korean Peninsula.

The optical screen showed the American continent. And right before our eyes, that entire continent was disappearing.

An enormous island rising from deep within the Earth.

As that island emerged, the entire American continent was being erased from the map.

[The probability of this world has reached its critical point!]

[The forgotten islands are beginning to rise!]

His face rigid, Yoo Joonghyuk murmured quietly.

“The apocalypse has begun.”
