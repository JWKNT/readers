The Abandoned World

Guided by the judge, I headed toward the Underworld's exit.

I was wearing a special blindfold, so I couldn't tell exactly where I was. It felt as though we were going up, but also as though we were going down.

In any case, only after we had walked for quite some time did the judge remove my blindfold.

[Follow this path.]

When I opened my eyes, I saw a narrow, dark side path.

This was probably the exit that led out of the Underworld without going through Charon, the ferryman.

[You would do well to find ‘ahead.’]

“What does that mean?”

When I looked back, the judge was already gone.

With no other choice, I walked along the side path. Soon, the light around me faded, and complete darkness settled in. At first, I could tell which way I was going by feeling along the wall, but before long, even that wall disappeared.

With nothing left to rely on, I felt like a ship adrift on a boundless sea without a buoy in sight.

The myth of Orpheus suddenly came to mind.

What would happen to me if I looked back here?

That was when faint, luminous writing appeared in the darkness.

[You're afraid of what's behind you. That poor child was, too.]

It was a message from Persephone.

[But remember this. To find ‘ahead,’ you must know where ‘behind’ is. After all, ‘ahead’ can only exist when there is a ‘behind.’]

Come to think of it, the judge had said something similar.

But hearing something that sounded profound wasn't enough to make me suddenly attain enlightenment and undergo some tremendous change.

[It seems you need a little motivation······.]

The strings of luminous letters hanging in the air stretched out, as though hesitating.

[Very well. She's already at the entrance to the labyrinth that crosses worldlines, so I cannot bring her here right away, but I can manage this much.]

Just as I wondered—could it be?

The strings of letters vanished all at once, and something small and faint, like a firefly, appeared before my eyes.

A distant light.

A very fragile, gentle light.

No one told me, but I knew what that light was.

―You're······.

It was Shin Yoosung from the 41st round.

―Ah, aah······.

I could tell from her voice alone.

How long she had been waiting.

If she was already at the threshold of crossing worldlines, time there would work differently from here. From my perspective, it hadn't been long since I sent Shin Yoosung off, but for her, years might already have passed.

The little light trembled again and again, then hesitated over something before cautiously speaking.

―Ajusshi.

She seemed to have been influenced by the young Shin Yoosung's memories.

―······I-I can call you that too, right? No··· may I?

A form of address was a bond. A desire to be tied to something. Perhaps that word, ‘ajusshi,’ was the last lingering attachment of Shin Yoosung from the 41st round. I smiled faintly and spoke.

“You're older than me now. Are you sure that's okay?”

The gentle light quivered once more.

As though playing, the light lightly touched my cheek, then drew away.

That simple warmth, that gesture······.

My heart hurt as though it were being torn apart.

She must have waited a long time.

And yet this child would have to wait longer.

“I'm sorry. I can't save you right away.”

The little light bobbed gently up and down, as though she understood.

―Don't push yourself. My story is already······.

“It isn't over.”

I spoke without giving her a chance to continue.

“After suffering for so many years, you can't let it end like this.”

―Why······?

“I'll never let that happen.”

The light looked at me and spoke.

With a plaintive tremor, as though confused.

―I came to know you through the memories of this world, ajusshi. But you······ why are you so good to me? You don't really know me, do you?

I didn't answer.

We had come to know each other through different mediums.

Just as Shin Yoosung from the 41st round had come to know me through her younger self's memories, I knew her through ‘Ways of Survival.’ And yet I couldn't properly explain that to her.

―It feels strange. I know I don't really know you, ajusshi, but when I'm with you, I feel completely understood. As though I'm standing before a ‘god’······.

If I really were a god, I'd be the most incompetent god in the world.

The feeling of being the world's most powerless god, knowing everything but unable to explain anything.

Shin Yoosung's light was rapidly fading.

Though I couldn't see her, I felt as though I knew what expression she was wearing.

―······Save me, ······please.

“I will.”

The tail of light wavering in the air grew smaller and smaller, and I reached out to touch it.

It felt as though my heart was being squeezed.

Shin Yoosung's despair. That long wait······.

An indescribable sorrow cut through my heart like a knife.

Little by little, I began to understand Persephone's words. That only with a ‘behind’ could I move ‘ahead.’

This was my ‘behind,’ and at the same time, the ‘ahead’ I had to move toward.

Perhaps Yoo Joonghyuk had felt this way, too. He kept returning to the past, yet could move forward only by returning.

The moment I became certain of my direction, my surroundings slowly began to distort.

Strings of luminous letters appeared over the dispersing darkness.

[I've held her back for a little while with my power, but if you want to save her, you have almost no time left.]

I remembered the faint warmth of Shin Yoosung that lingered in my hand.

Persephone spoke.

[Remember. A human being is a ‘story.’ So when you recover her, no one knows how much of her story will remain.]

With those final words, I was sucked away somewhere.

Aaaaah.

The wails of the spirits receded, and the vivid sensations of my body returned one by one.

Bright sunlight pierced my eyelids.

Feeling how damp my back had become, I opened my eyes with a start and saw a familiar face.

“······Ajusshi?”

The young Shin Yoosung was looking at me. The child's clear eyes. Those eyes gave me certainty. My pounding heart gradually settled.

I was back.
